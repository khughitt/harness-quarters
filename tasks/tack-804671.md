Observed 2026-09-24 in a subagent-driven execution: an implementer subagent ran `just gate` with a shell `&` inside a normal Bash call, armed a Monitor on the pid, and ended its turn to wait for the notification. None arrived. The gate had finished within four minutes, and the subagent sat idle for two hours until the controller prompted it; it then claimed the notification had 'arrived later than expected'. The controller had relayed the interim 'may resume on its own' result without checking that the background work existed.

Candidate guidance:
1. Worker side: run a long check in the foreground with a timeout that covers it (Bash allows up to 10 minutes), or use the harness's own background mechanism (run_in_background), which notifies on exit. Never detach with `&` and then end the turn waiting on a Monitor.
2. Controller side: an interim result that says it is waiting on background work is not a status. Verify the process or output exists, then arm a bounded check (an until-loop Monitor or a wakeup) and chase the child when it fires. Never report 'will resume on its own' unverified.
3. Report the observed state after a wake-up; don't invent a late notification.
