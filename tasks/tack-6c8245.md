Land the park-before-stopping obligation the approved obs charter assigns to `ai`: an agent may not end a turn with agent-owned work outstanding unless it has parked the task (naming the next owner and action) or completed the gate it was in, and agent-owned review stays agent-owned until an explicit handoff.

## Why

In the lit incident below, the agent ended its turn after committing Task 7 and before dispatching the independent reviewer it owed, described the pending review in present tense, and let the user infer they were the reviewer. Nothing in the flow skill or the global rules forbids stopping mid-state without a park, and `tasks` skill step 5 only requires a park when the turn *waits on the user*. The obs charter (`obs/docs/specs/2026-09-17-obs-evals-charter-design.md` §1, §4) detects violations from task parks and lifecycle evidence; it needs `ai` to obligate the behaviour so a missing park is a violation rather than a habit. `obs-b8accf` owns detection (`stalled` / `user-wait` / `unknown` findings); relay owns native child events; this task owns only the instruction.

## Done when

1. `agents/skills/flow/SKILL.md` carries a rule that `implementing` (and any other open state) may not end a turn without either a completed gate record or a `tasks park` — the park names the next owner and action (`--waiting-on user` only when the next step is genuinely the user's), and the closing message says execution has stopped. Agent-owned review (the `implementing → verified` judge) is dispatched by the agent, never described as pending for the user unless explicitly handed off with `--reason review`.
2. `AGENTS.md` (the global rules; `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` is this file) carries one bullet for non-flow work: a turn that ends with work outstanding says so in past tense — execution has stopped, why, and who owns the next step — and never reports an owed step as a wait on the user. Match the existing one-bullet rule style (see `734e9b2`, `a5ea4b3`); the recorded failure is this incident, which keeps the bullet alive under the `ai-9ec1eb` provenance audit.
3. `docs/specs/2026-09-15-flow-state-machine-design.md` no longer claims `flow` adds nothing to park (line 67ff.); it states the turn-end rule in the same change.
4. Wording is consistent with the obs stall query (§4 table): a park waiting on the agent is ordinary continuation, not an alert; a park waiting on the user shows the requested action; no park with outstanding work and a stopped session is the violation.

## Source material

Improve observability and user awareness when an agent stops making progress mid-task without clearly announcing that execution has stopped or what must happen next.

Reported failure modes to investigate:
- A subagent dies, stalls, or completes without notifying its parent orchestrator, leaving the parent waiting without a useful user-visible status.
- A required subagent step is never dispatched before the parent ends its turn, leaving unfinished agent-owned work presented as a pending gate.
- An implementation pauses for review without making clear whether the reviewer is another agent or the user. If external/user review is required, the user is not explicitly told that execution has stopped and what to review.

Concrete incident:
- Project: lit, checkout ~/d/lit, worktree .worktrees/world-model.
- Harness/model: Codex gpt-5.6-terra.
- Session: 01a0af15-98bd-7d83-8d44-56080d74f8e7.
- After 1h 06m 35s, the assistant said:

> Implementation is underway in .worktrees/world-model.
>
> Completed and reviewed through Task 6; Task 7 is committed (d4554da) and its focused 27-test suite passed. The full just check was running when this turn ended; Task 7 still needs its independent diff review before the planned capacity spike (Task 8).

- The user asked: "Great! Is the task still executing?"
- The assistant replied:

> No. The previous turn ended after Task 7’s commit; nothing is running now.
>
> The branch is at Task 7, awaiting its independent review before Task 8.

Follow-up supplied by the user:
- Asked what needed review, the assistant identified Task 7's committed diff: src/lit/world/build.py (production, citation-flow/drop accounting, exemplar tables, run_build), src/lit/world/cli.py (lit world build), and tests/test_build.py (aggregate/build-runner coverage).
- It listed eligibility buckets, flow totals/drop reasons, deterministic output/schema/compression, local-only inputs, and build identity/inventory handling as review criteria.
- The user volunteered to review, then asked why a subagent had not handled it.
- The assistant acknowledged:

> Yes—Task 7 should have been reviewed by a separate sub-agent, as Tasks 1–6 were. The prior turn ended after verification and review-package creation, before I dispatched that reviewer. There was no design reason to omit it; it was an incomplete SDD gate.

The assistant's follow-up identifies an omitted reviewer dispatch, not a failed or unresponsive reviewer, in this example. The agent stopped before completing an agent-owned workflow step and described the pending review without identifying its own responsibility; the user consequently volunteered to take over. Preserve this distinction from the separately reported dead-subagent/missing-notification failure mode. Session evidence should still verify the reported sequence and investigate why dispatch was skipped.

Desired outcomes (as originally captured):
- Users can distinguish active execution, a live wait on another agent/tool, a wait on the user, and stopped/failed execution.
- Detect or explicitly report required work that has not been dispatched, distinguishing it from a live wait or a failed child. A pending independent review must not silently become the user's responsibility.
- A review pause identifies the reviewer, the artifact and requested action, and whether anything remains running. Agent-owned review continues autonomously when already authorized.
- Lost/dead subagents and missing completion notifications become visible within a defined, bounded interval, with the affected session/task and a concrete recovery or escalation step.
- A turn ending with unfinished work clearly states that execution has stopped when that is true, why, and who owns the next step; present-tense progress language must not imply continued execution.
- Validate omitted reviewer dispatch, dead/unresponsive children or lost notifications, an explicit user-review pause, and a healthy long-running operation. Quiet but active work must not be mislabeled as stalled.

Original scoping note: inspect available lifecycle/session signals and existing status/notification mechanisms first; determine the smallest change and its owning layer before choosing polling, timeouts, hooks, or instruction changes. Keep implementation ownership across ai/relay/fam/ops open until evidence identifies it. (Resolved by the obs charter: ai owns the instruction.)

Related context: ai-bc49ed (session-log tooling), ai-9dfba9 (flow measurement), ai-21ea5d (workflow checkpoint hooks). These are related work, not established prerequisites.
