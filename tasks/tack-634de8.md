Outcome: a design spec in docs/specs that names the functional framing the flow, obs, relay and tasks work already share, so future work in those projects lands in the same shape. Core: pure derivations over evidence (flow-state, fingerprint, obs reconcile and cost attribution, tasks check, gate checks) with no IO. Shell: harness hooks, git, the filesystem, the model. Failures are values (a record, a diagnostic, a finding), never a swallowed exception or a silent fallback. Two effect kinds, relay's guard (may deny, fails closed) and observer (records only, failure isolated and reported). A gate is a typed record {artifact, check, judge, record}; a flow is an ordered list of gates with named back edges; composition is concatenation and nesting (a parent's state is a fold over its children, flow spec 3.3). Evidence classes observed / claimed / inferred (obs charter) type every derived figure. Typed means mechanically checked: a convention without a check is untyped. The judge at a gate is a bounded question with a typed verdict and its evidence (Jev's discipline); policy stays in the flow. Rejected: a work algebra or step-function library, implicit effects, a flow DSL before a second concrete flow exists (ai-da52b7 stays shelved). Approach: planned. Spec drafted in a task worktree, human review at the designed gate, then a short plan whose steps are the concrete pieces the framing exposes: the verified gate record carries a typed review verdict (findings count, disposition, reviewer identity) that obs can count; one documented gate-note grammar with a golden corpus that flow-state and obs GATE_RE both test against. Speculative consumers are filed as ideas beside the goal: obs review findings per task; a probe of Jev-style bounded reviewer questions against past verified gates. Verification: spec self-review (no placeholders; consistent with flow spec section 3 and the obs charter's evidence classes; every claim of 'already true' cites the file); human review at the designed gate; each plan step carries its own check.
